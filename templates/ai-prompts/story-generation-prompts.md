# AI Story Generation Prompts for Children's Books

This document contains proven prompts for generating high-quality children's stories using ChatGPT or Claude.

## Master Story Generation Prompt

Use this as your primary prompt, customizing the bracketed sections:

```
You are an award-winning children's book author specializing in stories for ages [3-7].

Create a children's book story with the following specifications:

THEME: [e.g., "learning to manage anger," "friendship," "bedtime routine"]
AGE GROUP: [3-5 or 5-7]
PAGE COUNT: [16, 24, or 32 pages]
EDUCATIONAL GOAL: [what should children learn?]
STYLE: [gentle and rhyming OR simple prose OR playful and rhythmic]

REQUIREMENTS:
1. Create a story with exactly [16/24/32] pages of text
2. Each page should have 1-3 sentences (max 25 words per page)
3. Use age-appropriate vocabulary (Flesch-Kincaid grade level: [K-2])
4. Include repetition and predictable patterns for engagement
5. Ensure diverse, inclusive characters
6. Include a clear beginning, middle, and satisfying end
7. Incorporate subtle educational message without being preachy
8. Make it emotionally resonant for both children and parents

FORMAT YOUR OUTPUT AS:
Page 1: [text]
[Image description: describe what illustration should show]

Page 2: [text]
[Image description: describe what illustration should show]

[Continue for all pages...]

Also provide:
- TITLE: [compelling, keyword-rich title]
- SUBTITLE: [clarifying subtitle]
- BACK COVER COPY: [2-3 sentence description for parents]
```

## Pre-Made Story Prompts by Category

### 1. Emotions & Feelings

#### Managing Anger
```
Create a 24-page children's book (ages 4-7) about a young [animal] who learns healthy ways to express and manage anger. The character should experience a relatable frustration (toy breaking, sibling conflict, etc.), try unhealthy responses first, then discover better coping strategies (deep breathing, talking about feelings, taking a break). End with the character successfully using these tools. Use simple rhyming text, max 20 words per page. Include diverse supporting characters.
```

#### Overcoming Fear
```
Write a 16-page bedtime story (ages 3-5) about a child afraid of the dark who discovers that nighttime is full of gentle, friendly things. Use soft, soothing language with repetitive phrases like "It's only..." Format: each page introduces a scary sound/shadow, next page reveals the harmless reality. Build confidence gradually. End with peaceful sleep. Max 15 words per page.
```

#### Celebrating Differences
```
Create a 24-page story (ages 5-7) about a group of diverse friends who each have unique abilities, appearances, or family structures. When a new child joins who seems very different, the group learns that differences make their friendship stronger. Avoid being preachy—show through fun activities how each friend's uniqueness contributes. Use conversational prose, max 25 words per page.
```

### 2. Educational Concepts

#### ABC Book
```
Create a 28-page ABC book (ages 2-4) with a unifying theme of [animals/food/vehicles/nature]. For each letter, provide:
- One simple sentence featuring the letter prominently (e.g., "A is for Alligator, snapping in the swamp")
- Alliteration when possible
- Engaging action or description
- Image description: vibrant, clear illustration of the subject

Format: A is for..., B is for..., etc. Include all 26 letters plus cover/title page.
```

#### Counting Book (1-10)
```
Write a 22-page counting book (ages 2-4) where a child encounters increasing numbers of [animals/objects] during [an adventure/daily routine]. For each number:
- Count items clearly: "One blue bird singing in the tree"
- Use rhythmic, repetitive structure
- Build a simple narrative arc
- Numbers 1-10, plus intro, ending, and title pages

Example structure:
Page 1: "One sunny morning, what do you see?"
Page 2: "ONE blue bird singing to me!"
Page 3: "Walking further, around the bend..."
Page 4: "TWO butterflies become my friends!"
```

#### Colors Book
```
Create a 16-page color exploration book (ages 2-4) where a character encounters different colors in nature/daily life. For each color, show:
- Objects of that color
- Associated emotions or feelings
- Interactive question ("Can you find something [color]?")
Use: Red, Blue, Yellow, Green, Orange, Purple, Pink, Brown, Black, White
Max 12 words per page. Make it interactive and engaging.
```

### 3. Daily Routines & Life Skills

#### Bedtime Routine
```
Write a 16-page bedtime story (ages 2-5) that models a calm bedtime routine. Follow a child and [parent/caregiver] through:
- Bath time
- Brushing teeth
- Pajamas
- Story time
- Hugs and kisses
- Lights out

Use soothing, repetitive language. Include a calming refrain like "Time to rest, time to sleep, time for dreams so soft and sweet." Max 18 words per page. End with peaceful sleeping image.
```

#### First Day of School
```
Create a 24-page story (ages 4-6) about a child's first day at [preschool/kindergarten]. Show realistic emotions: excitement turning to nervousness, then gradual comfort as the day progresses. Include:
- Saying goodbye to parent (brief sadness is okay)
- Meeting teacher
- Making a new friend
- Fun activities
- Reunion with parent at pickup
- Excitement about returning tomorrow

Use encouraging but honest tone. Don't minimize real fears. Max 22 words per page.
```

#### Potty Training
```
Write a 16-page potty training encouragement book (ages 2-4). Follow a young character learning to use the potty. Keep it:
- Positive and pressure-free
- Funny and relatable (accidents happen!)
- Step-by-step: feeling the urge, going to potty, celebration
- Inclusive of different family setups

Use cheerful, repetitive phrases. Include celebration without over-hyping. Max 15 words per page. Avoid shaming language entirely.
```

### 4. Adventure & Imagination

#### Animal Adventure
```
Create a 24-page adventure story (ages 4-7) where a child and their [animal friend] go on an imaginative journey to [location: forest, ocean, space, etc.]. Include:
- Clear beginning: introduction and inciting incident
- Middle: 3-4 challenges or discoveries
- Resolution: returning home with new wisdom/friendship
- Gentle educational element about [animals/nature/science]

Use vivid but simple language. Max 25 words per page. Make it exciting but not scary.
```

#### Magical Realism
```
Write a 24-page story (ages 5-7) where everyday objects come to life or have magical properties. A child discovers that [their stuffed animal/their garden/their drawings] have magic, and goes on an adventure. Keep magic rules consistent. Include:
- Discovery of magic
- Fun adventure using magic
- Small problem to solve with magic
- Return to normal with new appreciation

Use playful, wonder-filled language. Max 23 words per page.
```

## Story Structure Templates

### Classic Three-Act Structure
```
PAGES 1-4 (Setup):
- Introduce main character in their normal world
- Show their personality, interests, daily life
- Hint at the challenge or theme

PAGES 5-8 (Inciting Incident):
- Something changes or challenges the character
- Character has emotional response
- Decides to take action

PAGES 9-16 (Rising Action):
- Character tries to solve problem/learn lesson
- May fail first attempts (show resilience)
- Supporting characters help

PAGES 17-20 (Climax):
- Character faces biggest challenge
- Uses what they've learned
- Breakthrough moment

PAGES 21-24 (Resolution):
- Problem solved or lesson learned
- Character changed for the better
- Return to normal (but improved)
- Satisfying emotional closure
```

### Repetitive Pattern Structure (Younger Kids)
```
Perfect for ages 2-4. Use this structure:

PAGE 1-2: Introduction
- "Every morning, [Character] looks for..."

PAGES 3-16 (Repeat pattern):
- Page 3: "[Character] sees a [thing]. Is this it?"
- Page 4: "No! That's a [thing]!"
- Repeat with variations 6-7 times

PAGES 17-18: Discovery
- "[Character] finally finds it!"
- Celebration

PAGES 19-20: Ending
- Happy conclusion, bedtime, or return home
```

## Tips for Using These Prompts

1. **Be Specific:** The more details you provide, the better the output
2. **Iterate:** Generate 3-5 versions and combine the best elements
3. **Test Reading Level:** Use tools like Hemingway Editor to check grade level
4. **Read Aloud:** Stories should sound natural when read aloud
5. **Check Pacing:** Each page turn should feel natural, not rushed
6. **Image Descriptions:** Make them detailed for consistent illustration
7. **Get Feedback:** Test stories with real kids or parents before publishing

## Readability Targets by Age

- **Ages 2-3:** 5-10 words per page, simple nouns and verbs, repetition
- **Ages 4-5:** 10-20 words per page, simple sentences, some descriptive words
- **Ages 6-7:** 20-30 words per page, compound sentences okay, varied vocabulary

## Vocabulary Guidelines

### Ages 2-3 (Simple)
- Use: cat, dog, big, happy, mommy, go, see, play
- Avoid: complicated, frustrated, determined, wandered

### Ages 4-5 (Emerging)
- Use: excited, worried, friend, adventure, surprised, discovered
- Avoid: exasperated, contemplated, magnificent, extraordinary

### Ages 6-7 (Developing)
- Use: nervous, curious, realized, determined, adventure, mysterious
- Avoid: apprehensive, contemplative, serendipitous, melancholy

## Character Development Checklist

When creating your main character, ensure they have:
- [ ] Clear personality traits (curious, shy, energetic, etc.)
- [ ] Relatable problem or goal
- [ ] Emotional arc (how they change/grow)
- [ ] Distinctive appearance (for illustration consistency)
- [ ] Age-appropriate behavior and dialogue
- [ ] Universal appeal (not too niche)

## Story Testing Questions

Before finalizing your story, ask:
1. Does every page advance the story or develop the character?
2. Is the language age-appropriate and engaging?
3. Are there moments that make you smile or feel emotional?
4. Does the ending feel satisfying?
5. Would a child ask to read this again?
6. Does it respect children's intelligence while being accessible?
7. Are the illustrations clearly described?

## Advanced: Series Planning Prompt

```
I'm planning a children's book series called "[Series Name]" featuring [main character(s)].

SERIES CONCEPT:
- Target age: [age range]
- Main character(s): [names and brief description]
- Setting: [where stories take place]
- Theme: [overarching theme across books]

Generate 10 book ideas for this series. For each:
1. Book title
2. 2-sentence plot summary
3. Specific lesson or theme
4. How it connects to the series

Ensure variety in stories while maintaining consistent characters and world.
```
